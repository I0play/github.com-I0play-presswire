The Bench

Sep 12, 2026 · Electronics · ~8 minute read

That black iron tip is not worn out. You cooked the plating off and now you are soldering with a rock.

You crank the station to 450 °C because the joint would not wet. Two weeks later the tip is a charcoal stick that beads solder like a wet duck. You buy another cheap cone and repeat the funeral. The plating is iron over copper. Leave it dry and hot and the iron oxidizes. Oxide does not transfer heat. Then you scrape it with a file because a video said so, and you just removed the only layer that was supposed to survive.

A dead tip is almost never “used up.” It is abused. Temperature, dry heat, and abrasive cleaning kill more irons than actual solder joints.

Set a temperature that matches the work

Lead-free SAC305 wants more heat than 63/37. That is real. It does not want a blowtorch. For through-hole and most board work, 320 to 350 °C on a temperature-controlled station is plenty if the tip is tinned and the thermal mass matches the pad. Fine-pitch and small 0402 work can sit closer to 300. Big ground pours and connectors want a fatter tip at the same temperature, not a needle at 480.

Unregulated pencil irons lie. The number on the dial is a prayer. If the only tool you have is a dollar-store wand, tin constantly and get off the joint. Do not park it in the stand at full tilt for an hour while you hunt a resistor.

Tin is a coating, not a suggestion

A working tip is shiny. Before you put it in the stand, wipe, add a blob of fresh solder, then rack it. The solder is a sacrificial coat. It keeps air off the iron plating. A dry tip in a hot stand is an oxidation machine. Brass wool is for wiping the excess so you can see the work. It is not a grinding wheel. If you have to scrub for ten seconds, the tip is already gone and you are polishing oxide.

Tip tinner — the little tin of abrasive flux and solder powder — is a rescue, not a daily soap. Use it when the wetability dies and a wipe will not bring it back. One dip, wipe on brass, re-tin with your regular alloy. Do this twice. If it is still black, the plating is through. Replace the tip. Filing a plated tip is how you expose the copper core. Copper dissolves into solder and the tip becomes a pit.

The sponge is not a lake

A dripping cellulose sponge quenches the tip. Thermal shock flakes plating. If you insist on a sponge, it should be damp, not swimming. Brass wool in a coil does the same cleaning job without the ice-bath. Do not use steel wool. Do not use sandpaper. Do not use the edge of a screwdriver because the pad looked stubborn.

Flux does the wetting. Heat just delivers it.

If a joint will not take solder, more temperature is the last lever, not the first. Fresh flux on the pad, a clean tinned tip, contact both the pad and the lead, feed solder into the joint not onto the iron. Two seconds. Pull away. A dull lumpy fillet on lead-free can still be fine. A ball that sits on top of a pad means you never heated the copper. Cranking heat until the mask blisters is how you lift pads and still get a cold joint.

Old solder on the tip is dirty. Wipe it, add a little fresh wire, then go to the board. The flux in the core is a one-shot. Burned flux on a tip is carbon. Carbon is an insulator. That is why yesterday’s blob does not help today’s joint.

Match the tip to the thermal load

A needle tip on a USB connector ground lug is a joke. The copper pour steals the heat and you sit there for eight seconds while the plating cooks. Chisel or hoof, sized so the face covers most of the pad. For drag solder on a row of pins, a small hoof beats a cone. Keep two or three shapes and swap them. Tips are cheaper than lifted pads and cheaper than a new station you will also ruin.

A bench checklist

  1. Park the station at 320–350 °C for normal board work. Raise heat only after flux and tip size fail.
  2. Wipe, tin, rack. A dry hot tip is how plating dies.
  3. Brass wool, damp sponge at most. No files, no sandpaper, no screwdriver.
  4. Tip tinner is a rescue. Two tries, then replace the tip.
  5. Use a chisel or hoof on anything with copper mass. Needles are for needles.

A shiny tip at a sane temperature outsolders a black spike at 450 every day of the week. Treat the plating like a cutting edge. Once you grind it off, you do not get it back.
