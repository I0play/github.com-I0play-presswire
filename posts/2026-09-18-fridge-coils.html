That fridge runs all afternoon because the coils on the back are a dust filter, not a radiator.

The compressor is loud. The sides are warm. Milk lasts four days. You start shopping for stainless. Unplug it and look behind or underneath. Those black grid fins are how the box dumps heat into the room. They are also a magnet for lint, pet hair, and the last three years of kitchen grease. A packed condenser makes the stack run hot, the duty cycle go stupid, and the electric meter smile.

This is not a sealed-system job. This is a coil brush, a vacuum, and enough space behind the cabinet that air can actually move. Do it twice a year and the “new fridge” urge usually dies.

Find the coils before you buy a brush for the wrong end

Older and a lot of basic machines hang the condenser on the back like a black radiator. Pull the fridge straight out. Do not walk it on the front feet or you crease the vinyl and pinch the water line if you have an ice maker. Unplug it. The grid should look like metal, not felt.

Many bottom-freezer and counter-depth boxes hide the condenser under a kick plate. Pop the grille. You will see a fan, a drain pan, and a coil pack packed with gray wool. That fan is not optional. If it is seized or full of dust, the compressor cooks even if you wipe the back panel and call it done.

Do not jam the coil brush into the evaporator behind the freezer panel. That is a different coil, usually frosted on purpose, and you will bend fins and stab a tube. Stay on the warm side of the system.

Clean like you mean the heat to leave

Vacuum first with a brush attachment so you are not grinding grit into the fins. Then a condenser coil brush — long, thin, stiff nylon — between the rows. Work top to bottom. Vacuum again. A rag on the painted cabinet is fine. A rag mashed into the fins just polishes the lint in place.

Underneath, pull the dust from the fan shroud and the drain pan. The pan should be damp at most. A swamp is a drain problem, not a coil problem. If the fan blade hits the shroud, the mount walked. Snug it. A screaming fan is not “how fridges sound.”

Leave the machine unplugged a few minutes after you move it so the oil in the compressor settles before you restart. Superstition with a little physics. Then plug in and listen. The first run after a clean should not scream.

Give it air or the clean coils still bake

The manual wants space. An inch at the sides and a couple at the back is not interior design, it is the chimney. A fridge shoved under a soffit with no gap and a toaster on top is a box trying to dump heat into a closet. If you must go tight, you need the model that was built for it, not a hope.

Level it. A fridge that leans hard forward does not drain the defrost water and the door does not seal. The front feet adjust. A marble on the top shelf is a cheap inclinometer. The door gasket should kiss the cabinet all the way around. A dollar bill that slides out with no drag is a leak. Warm kitchen air in means the stack runs longer. Clean coils will not save a chewed gasket.

When cleaning is not the fix

If the freezer is a block of ice and the fridge is warm, you are looking at airflow inside — a failed evaporator fan or a jammed damper — not the condenser. If the compressor clicks every few minutes and never stays on, the overload is hot or the start device is toast. Those are parts. If you hear sloshing and see oil stains on the floor under the back, that is a leak and you are shopping, not brushing.

A fridge that is empty and opened forty times on a holiday will run. That is load. A fridge that runs in a 90 °F garage will run. That is ambient. Clean coils still help. They do not repeal thermodynamics.

A kitchen checklist

  1. Unplug. Pull straight. Find the condenser — back grid or under the kick plate.
  2. Vacuum, coil-brush the fins, vacuum again. Clear the fan and the pan.
  3. Leave gap behind and beside. Level the cabinet. Check the door gasket.
  4. Do not stab the freezer evaporator. That is the cold coil.
  5. If it still short-cycles or the floor is oily, stop brushing and call a tech or price a box.

Heat has to leave. Dust is insulation you did not order. Ten minutes with a brush is cheaper than a compressor and quieter than a fridge that never shuts up.
