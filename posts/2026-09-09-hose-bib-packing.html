If the outdoor faucet weeps around the stem when it is open, the packing is tired. A wrench beats a new sillcock.

Water at the spout when the handle is closed is a seat or a washer. Water sneaking out around the stem while the hose is running is packing. People buy a whole frost-free faucet because a drip looks dramatic. Half the time the packing nut is a quarter-turn loose from last winter and the graphite ring is dry.

Packing is the compressible stuff under the nut behind the handle. It squeezes around the stem so pressure cannot follow the threads to daylight. Heat, UV, and a handle that got yanked with a pair of pliers cook it. The leak shows up the first warm Saturday you actually use the hose.

Confirm it is packing, not the spout

Open the bib. Watch the stem, not the hose. A bead at the packing nut or a wet handle is packing. A drip from the spout after you shut it off is the seat. Two different repairs. Do not wrap the whole faucet in PTFE tape and call it a day. Tape on the hose threads will not touch a stem leak.

If the faucet is frost-free, the seat lives inside the wall on a long stem. You can still service packing from outside. You cannot “just snug it forever” if the stem is scored. Look once.

Snug first

Shut the interior valve if you have one. You can often work a packing nut with the house valve open if you are quick, but a wet sleeve is a stupid tax. Pull the handle screw. Slide the handle off so you can get a wrench on the packing nut — the small nut under the handle, not the big body-to-pipe joint.

Turn the packing nut clockwise a fraction. Eighth of a turn. Open the faucet. Dry the stem. Wait. If the weep stops, stop turning. Packing that is crushed to death makes the handle a workout and still leaks next season. You want squeeze, not a welded stem.

A drop of plumber’s grease on the stem threads after you back the nut off a hair helps the next person. Do not pack grease into a drinking-water aerator. This is the stem, outdoors, under a nut.

When snug is not enough

The nut bottoms and it still weeps. Replace the packing. Some bibs take a packing washer. Some take graphite string or PTFE packing you wind with the lay of the stem, a couple of turns, cut clean, nut back on. Do not use random string from a shop rag. Do not use a fat O-ring that does not belong in that bore.

Match what came out. If you find a flat fiber washer, buy that size. If you find black string, buy packing material sold for faucets and valves. Wind it so tightening the nut packs it inward, not so it unravels when you turn the handle.

A deeply scored stem will cut new packing in a week. Then you replace the stem or the whole sillcock. That is the honest fork. A $4 packing job is still the first move.

Frost-free is not magic

A frost-free hose bib shuts off inside the heated envelope. The pipe in the wall drains if you disconnect the hose in winter. Leave a pressure washer or a shut-off nozzle hanging on the threads and the water in the body cannot drain. Then January does the demolition. Disconnect the hose. Open the bib after you shut the interior valve so the stem bore empties.

If the frost-free drips from the spout in summer after shutoff, the washer at the far end of the long stem is shot. That is a stem kit, not packing. Same handle removal. The stem unscrews from the body. Do not crank the body in the wall while you fight a seized stem. Back the interior valve off, cut the water, and use two wrenches if the sillcock starts to rotate.

What not to do

Do not crank the big wall flange nuts because the handle drips. Those nuts hold pipe, not packing. Do not seal the leak with silicone on the stem. You will glue the handle in place and still have water in the wall. Do not ignore a leak that runs along the siding. Water behind clapboard is a rot project with a longer invoice than a packing nut.

A yard checklist

  1. Open the faucet. If it weeps at the stem, it is packing. If it drips at the spout after close, it is the seat.
  2. Pull the handle. Snug the packing nut an eighth-turn. Test. Stop when it is dry.
  3. Still wet: new packing or washer that matches what came out. Grease the stem lightly.
  4. Scored stem or seized frost-free rod: replace the stem kit or the sillcock. Do not twist the pipe in the wall.
  5. Winter: hose off, interior valve shut, bib open so the body can drain.

A packing nut is a service part. Treat it like one. The new faucet can wait until the old body is actually finished.
