If the tank refills every twenty minutes with nobody home, you are flushing money into the sewer.

A running toilet is not a personality. It is a flapper that no longer seals, a fill valve that no longer stops, or a chain that is short enough to hold the flapper cracked open. The tank hisses. The bowl has a permanent trickle. Your water bill grows a new digit and you blame the teenager.

This is a one-hour job with a $20 kit if you do not overthink it. It is a weekend flood if you wrench on the supply line like it owes you rent.

Find which part is lying

Lift the tank lid. Do not drop it. Mark the water line with a pencil if you want a reference. Flush. Watch.

If the flapper drops and the water line keeps falling, the flapper or the flush-valve seat is leaking into the bowl. Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is a sieve.

If the tank overfills and water goes down the overflow tube, the fill valve is not shutting off. The float is set too high, the valve is worn, or the refill tube is shoved down the overflow like a straw. Pull it out so it only clips to the rim of the overflow. It should dribble into the tube, not pressurize it.

If the chain is taut when the flapper is down, shorten the slack problem: add a link of slack so the flapper can sit flat. A chain that wraps the flapper hinge will hold it open forever and you will swear the new flapper is defective.

Shut the water off like an adult

The angle stop under the tank should close with a quarter-turn or a few turns of a multi-turn valve. If it crumbles or leaks at the packing, stop and replace the stop later. Do not keep twisting. Flush to empty the tank. Sponge out the rest so you are not working in a bath.

Have a towel and a small bucket. The supply nut will drip. That is not a crisis. Leaving the water on while you yank the fill valve is how you redecorate the vanity.

Flapper first, because it is cheap

Unhook the chain. Peel the old flapper off the ears of the flush valve or slide it off the overflow. Take it to the store if the shape is weird. Universal flappers lie; some 3-inch flush valves need a 3-inch flapper, not the 2-inch hockey puck in the impulse bin.

Wipe the seat with a Scotch-Brite pad. Mineral crust on the seat is why a brand-new flapper still leaks. Seat the new flapper, hook the chain with a little slack, and fill. Listen. No hiss after the tank stops is the whole grade.

Replace the fill valve when the float is a fossil

A ballcock with a corroded float rod is not worth rebuilding. Buy a modern tower fill valve. Note the tank height. Cut the valve stem to the mark so the critical level is an inch below the top of the overflow.

Under the tank, hold the valve body so it does not spin, crack the supply nut, then the locknut. Lift the old valve out. Clean the hole. Drop the new valve in, rubber gasket inside the tank, locknut snug — snug, not “I work at a tire shop.” Reattach the supply. Clip the refill tube to the overflow. Turn water on slowly. Set the water line about 1/2 inch below the top of the overflow with the adjuster on the valve, not by bending a rod until it snaps.

If the tank-to-bowl bolts are rusty flakes, replace them while you are in there. A leaking tank bolt is a slow rot of the floor, not a character feature.

How you know you missed it

  • Color still shows in the bowl after a dye test.
  • Water crawls down the overflow after the valve “shuts off.”
  • The fill never quite stops and the valve hums.
  • You hear the tank top off at 2 a.m. when the house is dead quiet.

A habit for the next bathroom

  1. Dye-test once a year. It is free and it catches a $200 water bill early.
  2. Keep a flapper and a fill valve on the shelf. They are not vintage wine.
  3. Do not overtighten tank bolts or china becomes gravel.
  4. If the flush valve seat itself is chewed up, replace the whole flush-valve assembly or the toilet. A flapper cannot seal a crater.

A quiet tank after a flush is the sound of a seal that works. Everything else is a leak you have agreed to fund.
