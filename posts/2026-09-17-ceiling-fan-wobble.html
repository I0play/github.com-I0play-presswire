That fan is not haunted. A blade sits low, a screw backed out, and the box is walking in the joist.

You crank it to three and the light kit draws a circle on the ceiling. You tape a coin to a blade because a video said so. The wobble moves to a new phase and the canopy still clicks. Balance kits fix ounces. They do not fix a fan that is hanging from two drywall screws and a prayer.

A ceiling fan is a rotating mass on a downrod. If the axis is not straight, if one blade flies a different pitch, or if the box is loose, you get a shake that loosens everything else. Fix structure first. Stick-on weights last.

Kill power and shake the housing

Breaker off. The wall switch is not enough if someone else lives here. Stand on a real ladder. Grab the motor housing and try to rock it. Play at the canopy means the box or the bracket is moving. Play at a blade iron means a screw is loose. No play and a clean spin later means you are in balance territory.

Fan-rated box. That is not a slogan. A plastic old-work box with spring ears is not a fan box. If you see the ears walking or the plaster cracked in a ring, stop decorating and replace the support. Brace between joists or a listed fan box with wood behind it. A 20-pound motor at speed will oval cheap screws in a season.

Downrod and canopy

The downrod pin and the set screw both have jobs. Missing pin is how a fan becomes a floor tile. Tight set screw into the rod dimple, not into paint. Canopy screws snug so the plate cannot rattle against the ceiling. A gap that opens and closes while it runs is the box moving. You will not tape that shut.

If you shortened a rod or reused a sloped-ceiling kit on a flat ceiling, check that the hanger ball sits in the bracket and can rotate a little. A ball cocked in the groove throws the whole axis off. Seat it. Listen. A grind at the ball is metal on a bad seat, not a “need more oil” situation.

Blades are not optional trim

Every blade-iron screw into the motor and every blade-to-iron screw. Hand tight plus a snug quarter. Stripped particle-board blades get a toothpick and glue or a new blade set. Mixing one replacement blade from a different year is how you buy a balance kit that never wins.

Measure blade height at the tip from the ceiling. One low blade is a bent iron or a motor-hub screw that was never seated. Swap two blades and see if the low spot moves. If it stays on the iron, the iron is bent. Gentle persuasion close to the hub, not a heroic twist at the tip that cracks the foil.

Dust on the top of the blades is mass. Uneven dust is uneven mass. Wipe all of them. This is not a joke. A season of fuzz on one blade and a clean opposite blade is a weight you already own.

Balance kit without folklore

After the hardware is tight and the tips fly in one plane, use the clip. Put it on the trailing edge of one blade, run medium speed, move it blade to blade. The quietest blade gets the weight on that centerline, then slide in and out along the blade to refine. One weight. Maybe two. A ring of coins is a confession that the hub is still cocked.

Do not balance on high first. High masks a lot and stresses a loose box. Medium tells the truth. Stand out from under it while you test. A blade that lets go does not send a warning text.

Noise that is not wobble

A tick once per rev is often a blade kissing a pull chain or a glass shade off-center. A buzz that follows dimmer angle is the fan on a lighting dimmer. Fans want a listed fan control or a wall switch. Light-kit dimmers stay on the lamps. Mixing them is how you buy a humming motor and a warranty argument.

A ladder checklist

  1. Breaker off. Confirm the box is fan-rated and actually tight to framing.
  2. Pin and set screw in the downrod. Canopy snug, hanger ball seated.
  3. Every blade and iron screw. Tips the same distance from the ceiling.
  4. Wipe the blades. Then clip-balance on medium if it still walks.
  5. Fan-rated control for the motor. Leave lighting dimmers on the lamps.

A wobble is almost never a ghost in the windings. It is loose hardware, a bent iron, or a box that should have been replaced before the second summer. Tighten the axis. Then add the gram of tape.
