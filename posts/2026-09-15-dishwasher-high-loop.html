The dishwasher is not filling itself with sink water. Your drain hose is a downhill straw into the disposal.

You open the door and the bottom is a gray pond that smells like last night’s pasta. The machine ran. The float did not stick. The supply valve is fine. The hose from the dishwasher to the disposal tailpiece droops in a U under the sink and sits lower than the tub. Gravity and a garbage disposal full of wet food do the rest. Dirty water walks back into the machine every time the sink drains or the disposal runs.

This is not a control-board mystery. It is a hose path. Codes and manufacturers want a high loop or an air gap so the sink cannot siphon into the washer. Skip that and you own a free sample of the drain.

High loop versus air gap

A high loop is the drain hose strapped up under the countertop, as high as it will go, before it drops to the disposal inlet or the wye on the sink tailpiece. The peak is above the flood level of the sink. Water would rather fall into the drain than climb into the tub.

An air gap is the chrome hat on the deck with two hoses under it. Dishwasher into the gap, gap into the disposal. If the drain clogs, the mess dumps on the counter instead of into the clean dishes. Some cities require the hat. Some people hate the look and use a loop where the code allows it. Either one beats a hose lying in the cabinet floor.

Do both and you just added fittings. Pick the one your jurisdiction and the machine manual agree on. Then actually fasten it. A loop that sags after the first grocery bag under the sink is not a loop.

Where it ties in

Most disposals have a dishwasher nipple on the side. Knock out the plug from inside the grind chamber before you clamp the hose. Leave the plug in and the dishwasher pumps against a wall. People replace pumps over that. Fish the knockout out with tongs so it does not live in the impeller.

No disposal: use a tailpiece wye above the trap, not a stab into the trap arm with a cheater tee that sits full of water. The connection must be above the trap weir or you are building a second trap and a stink. Clamp with a real stainless worm-gear clamp. The spring toy in the box is a suggestion that loosens.

Symptoms that are the hose, not the pump

Standing water after a cycle that used to empty. Grit on the bottom that looks like coffee and onion. A sink that gurgles when the dishwasher drains. Disposal juice in the next clean load. A hose that is warm and fat after a drain attempt because it is kinked behind the kick plate.

Pull the machine if you have to. Look at the run from the tub outlet, through the cabinet, to the sink. No sags on the cabinet floor. No crush where the dishwasher slides in. No sharp bend at the disposal nipple. Replace a collapsed corrugated hose. That ribbed junk pinches and never forgets.

Air gap maintenance is ugly and short

The chrome cap unscrews. Inside is a plastic body that collects sludge. If the dishwasher backs up onto the counter through the gap, the downstream hose is clogged, not the machine. Pull the hose at the disposal, clear the gunk, run a cycle with the door closed and a towel ready. If water still dumps at the gap, the disposal inlet or the branch is blocked. Fix that before you order a new pump.

Do not cap an air gap and call it a remodel. You just forced the failure into the tub.

While you are under there

Check the supply. Braided line, working shutoff, no cardboard washer pretending to be a gasket. Check the float in the tub. A stuck float will refuse to fill or refuse to drain depending on the model, but it will not explain dirty sink water in a clean load. That is still the hose.

Run the disposal with a sink full of water after you reconnect. Watch the high loop. It should jump and then sit empty-ish. If it stays a hose-shaped balloon, the outlet is restricted.

A under-sink checklist

  1. Hose peaks under the countertop or through a real air gap. Nothing draped on the cabinet floor.
  2. Disposal nipple open. Knockout removed and fished out. Clamp tight on a barb, not on air.
  3. Tie-in above the trap. No cheater under water.
  4. No crush behind the dishwasher. Replace collapsed corrugation.
  5. Test: empty sink, run drain, then run the tap. The tub should not gain gray water.

Dirty water in a dishwasher is usually plumbing geometry, not a haunted control. Lift the hose, open the knockout, clamp it like you mean it. The pump cannot fight a straw into the disposal.
